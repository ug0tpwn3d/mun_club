Resolution Writing.

SKIT MUN Adjudication Core · Standard Drafting Manual

A resolution is the culminating achievement of a Model United Nations committee. It transforms hours of spirited debate, cross-regional lobbying, and compromise into a formal, binding or recommendatory international document.

1. Sponsors vs. Signatories

Before submitting a draft resolution to the Executive Board dais for formal recognition:

  • Sponsors: The principal authors who drafted the operational language and fundamentally agree with every clause in the document.
  • Signatories: Member states that wish to see the draft resolution introduced on the floor for substantive debate, without necessarily endorsing every proposal.

2. Preambulatory Clauses (The "Why")

Preambulatory clauses outline the historical backdrop, acknowledge past treaties, and express concern or conviction. They begin with italicized present participles and end with a comma:

  • Affirming the principles enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations,
  • Deeply concerned by the proliferation of illicit small arms in post-conflict borders,
  • Recalling General Assembly Resolution 74/180 regarding peaceful dispute resolution,

3. Operative Clauses (The "What" and "How")

Operative clauses prescribe actionable, concrete solutions. They are numbered sequentially, begin with an italicized active verb in the present tense, and end with a semicolon (with the final clause ending in a period):

  1. Urges all Member States to implement transparent oversight mechanisms on cross-border arms transshipments;
  2. Calls upon international development banks to allocate humanitarian grant facilities for affected civic communities;
  3. Decides to remain actively seized of the matter.

4. The Amendment Process

Once introduced, delegates may propose Amendments to add, delete, or modify specific operative clauses. Amendments can be Friendly (approved by all sponsors without a vote) or Unfriendly (voted upon by the committee before final resolution passage).
