Diplomatic Writing · Policy Briefs

Position Papers.

SKIT MUN Editorial Desk · Standard Policy Guide

A Position Paper is a concise, one-to-two page policy document that encapsulates your assigned nation's stance on the committee agenda. It represents your analytical roadmap for debate and is mandatory for Best Delegate evaluation.

1. Three-Part Standard Architecture

Every professional diplomatic brief should be divided into three clear operational sections:

  • Section I: Background & Problem Analysis: Contextualize the agenda globally. How does the conflict or crisis impact regional stability, human rights, or international commerce?
  • Section II: National Policy & Historical Actions: What domestic legislation, UN treaties (e.g., Geneva Conventions, Non-Proliferation Treaty), or bilateral aid packages has your country enacted?
  • Section III: Proposed Multilateral Solutions: Practical, realistic, and creative mechanisms your nation intends to champion in draft resolutions during unmoderated caucuses.

2. Formatting & Citation Standards

Ensure your document adheres to standard formatting: 1-inch margins, 11pt readable typography, official delegation header (Committee, Country, Agenda, Delegate Name), and precise citations of UN resolutions (e.g., UN General Assembly Resolution A/RES/68/262).

3. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Never write in first-person ("I believe"); always write from the perspective of your sovereign state ("The Delegation of Japan reaffirms..."). Avoid vague assertions without data or proposing solutions that contradict your country's documented geopolitical alliances.
