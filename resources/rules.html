Rules of Procedure.

Parliamentary procedure is not designed to restrict debate; it is the framework that enables sixty sovereign voices to reach democratic consensus under strict time limits.

1. The Four Essential Points

Points are procedural mechanisms raised by delegates to address operational matters without entering substantive debate:

  • Point of Personal Privilege: Raised due to physical discomfort impairing participation (inaudibility, room temperature, technical issues). May interrupt a speaker only if audio is inaudible.
  • Point of Order: Raised when the Executive Board or a delegate commits an improper parliamentary error regarding the Rules of Procedure.
  • Point of Parliamentary Inquiry: A clarifying question posed to the Chair regarding the current procedural state of the floor.
  • Point of Information: A substantive question directed to a delegate who has yielded the floor to questions following their speech.

2. Motions and Debate Flow

When the floor is declared open by the Chair, delegates may raise placards to introduce motions:

  • Motion for a Moderated Caucus: Pauses the GSL to focus debate on a specific agenda facet. Must specify total duration, speaking time per delegate, and exact topic.
  • Motion for an Unmoderated Caucus: Temporarily suspends formal rules, allowing delegates to move freely, lobby across regional blocs, and draft resolution clauses.
  • Motion to Table or Adjourn Debate: Suspends discussion on the current draft resolution or closes the committee session.

3. The Resolution Lifecycle & Voting Bloc

Debate progresses through distinct stages: Working Papers (informal proposals) - Draft Resolutions (formally recognized documents with sponsors and signatories) - Amendments (friendly or unfriendly modifications) - Voting Bloc (doors sealed, no entry/exit, voting on final passage).
