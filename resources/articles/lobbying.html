Delegate Resources · Category G: Negotiation

Lobbying & Bloc Formation.

By SKIT MUN Editorial Team · 8 Min Read

While formal speeches establish public positions, the real architecture of a UN resolution is negotiated on the committee floor during unmoderated caucuses. Master the art of lobbying to lead your bloc.

1. What Is a Bloc in Model United Nations?

A bloc is an alliance of member states sharing geographical proximity, ideological principles, or mutual geopolitical interests. Typical geopolitical blocs include:

  • Western European and Others Group (WEOG): Prioritizes human rights safeguards, liberal democratic governance, and transparent funding accountability.
  • Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) / Group of 77 (G77): Champions sovereignty, non-intervention, South-South trade cooperation, and climate debt reparations.
  • African Union (AU) Caucus: Focuses on regional peace enforcement, developmental autonomy, agricultural subsidies, and capacity building.
  • Small Island Developing States (SIDS): Focuses intensely on existential climate change, sea-level rise, maritime exclusive economic zones, and disaster relief.

2. Executing the First Unmoderated Caucus

The first unmoderated caucus is the most decisive 20 minutes of the entire conference:

  • Immediate Gathering Point: Step to an open corner or whiteboard in the chamber immediately upon the motion passing. Call out to your natural regional partners.
  • Appoint Scribes and Sub-Working Groups: Do not allow 15 delegates to talk simultaneously over one laptop screen. Divide your bloc into two thematic sub-groups (e.g., Funding & Logistics vs. Enforcement & Legal Frameworks).
  • Value Quiet Contributors: Check in with newer delegates standing on the perimeter. Assign them specific clause tasks; this builds unshakeable loyalty and secures their votes during resolution voting.

3. Sponsor vs. Signatory Dynamics

Understanding the procedural difference is crucial for committee leadership:

  • Sponsors (Usually 2 to 5 delegations): The principal authors who drafted the operational clauses, defended the working paper before the committee, and pledged to vote in favor. Chairs judge primary leadership based on sponsorship.
  • Signatories: Delegations who wish to see the draft resolution formally introduced to the floor for debate, regardless of whether they agree with all provisions. Securing signatories requires persuasion without giving away authorship rights.

4. Defending Against Bloc Poaching

Rival blocs will attempt to peel away your signatories by offering last-minute amendments. Keep your coalition united by maintaining radical transparency: share your working document live, credit every contributing country by name during GSL speeches, and hold brief 30-second huddles before each voting session.
