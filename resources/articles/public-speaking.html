Public Speaking & GSL.

In a room of sixty delegates competing for the Executive Board's attention, vocal eloquence without policy substance is hollow, and substance without rhetorical command is ignored. Here is how to master the podium.

1. The 3-Part Speech Architecture

Every 60-to-90 second GSL or moderated caucus speech should follow a rigorous tripartite framework:

  • The Hook (15s): Grab attention immediately with an undeniable statistic, historical precedent, or geopolitical friction point. Avoid generic opening pleasantries.
  • The Substance (45s): State your nation's sovereign policy. Explain what action your country has taken domestically or multilateral frameworks you champion.
  • The Call to Action (20s): Explicitly invite like-minded delegations to consult with your bloc during the upcoming unmoderated caucus.

2. Non-Verbal Presence & Placard Discipline

Maintain steady eye contact across the three sectors of the chamber—left bloc, right bloc, and the Executive Board dais. Speak with an unhurried, measured pace. Silence and intentional pauses emphasize crucial words far better than raised volume.

3. Yielding the Floor Strategically

Always manage your remaining seconds intentionally:

  • Yield to the Chair: Standard conclusion when remaining time is under 10 seconds.
  • Yield to Points of Information (Questions): Demonstrates intellectual confidence and invites rebuttal from other delegates.
  • Yield to Another Delegate: High-level tactical maneuver to allow a bloc ally to speak and strengthen your shared resolution.
