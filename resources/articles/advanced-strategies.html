Delegate Resources · Category H: Advanced Strategy

Advanced MUN Strategy & Tactics.

By SKIT MUN Editorial Team · 10 Min Read

Moving from a competent participant to a gavel-winning delegate requires mastering the fine procedural levers of committee flow: strategic motions, amendment maneuvers, and coalition preservation.

1. Setting the Committee Tempo: Motion Precedence

A master delegate controls the clock. When the Executive Board calls for points or motions, understand the hierarchy of precedence:

  • Points Over Motions: Point of Personal Privilege (audibility), followed by Point of Order (procedural rule infraction), and Point of Parliamentary Inquiry (asking the chair for clarification).
  • Suspension vs. Adjournment: A motion to suspend the meeting for a moderated caucus takes precedence over general debate motions.
  • Moderated Caucus Duration Strategy: Proposing a targeted 9-minute caucus with 45-second speaking times forces rapid, high-density contributions, preventing meandering speeches and allowing your bloc to dominate speaking slots.

2. The Strategic Friendly vs. Unfriendly Amendment Game

Amendments alter the operational language of an approved draft resolution:

  • Friendly Amendments: Approved unanimously by all primary sponsors of the draft resolution. These are integrated directly into the text without requiring committee debate or voting. Use friendly amendments to absorb neutral uncommitted delegations into your coalition.
  • Unfriendly Amendments: Proposed by delegates outside your bloc and contested by at least one primary sponsor. These require formal signatures, dedicated debate (typically two speakers in favor, two against), and a substantive vote. Skillful delegates use unfriendly amendments to neutralize hostile clauses before the final resolution vote.

3. Division of the Question

During the final voting bloc, if a draft resolution contains 10 brilliant clauses and 1 controversial clause that threatens its passage, any delegate may move for a Division of the Question:

  • The motion votes whether to sever the controversial clause and vote on it independently.
  • If the severance motion passes, delegates vote separately on the contested section.
  • This procedural tool preserves the core legislation while safely discarding toxic provisions.

4. The Art of the Right of Reply

If an opposing delegate directly slanders the sovereign dignity or national honor of your country during formal debate, you may submit an immediate written request to the Dais for a Right of Reply. Keep it brief, factual, dignified, and devastating. Never use offensive language; let verified international law and UN records refute personal characterizations.
