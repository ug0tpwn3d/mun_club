Country Research & Policy Dossier.

In Model United Nations, superior debate originates not from theatrical delivery, but from rigorous, fact-based policy preparation. To persuade sovereign nations, you must know your portfolio inside out.

1. The Three Pillars of MUN Research

Effective delegate research balances three interrelated dimensions:

  • Macro National Profile: Geography, governance, economic fundamentals (GDP, primary export commodities, sovereign debt), military alliances, and major bilateral trade treaties.
  • Specific Agenda Context: How the committee topic impacts your nation domestically, whether your nation is a contributor or a recipient of international aid, and historical vulnerability indicators.
  • Multilateral Diplomatic History: Speeches made by your head of state at the UN General Assembly, treaties signed or ratified, and voting records on relevant Security Council or General Assembly resolutions.

2. Primary vs. Secondary Sources

A common mistake among first-time delegates is relying entirely on encyclopedia articles or non-peer-reviewed blog posts. The Executive Board rewards delegates who cite authoritative, primary international documents:

  • UN Digital Library (digitallibrary.un.org): The definitive archive of official UN voting records, speeches, and draft resolutions.
  • UN Treaty Collection (treaties.un.org): Verifies whether your country ratified, signed with reservations, or rejected specific conventions.
  • Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA) Press Statements: Offers the authentic diplomatic vocabulary and official ministerial stances of your assigned country.
  • World Bank & IMF Databanks: Provides undeniable statistical evidence for socioeconomic arguments, debt relief, and development financing.

3. Building the 1-Page Research Dossier

During committee, flipping through a hundred-page binder wastes critical seconds. Synthesize your research into a clean, modular one-page briefing card:

[NATION]: Republic of Indonesia
[BLOCS]: ASEAN, Non-Aligned Movement (NAM), G20, OIC
[CORE INTEREST]: Maritime security, archipelagic sovereignty, sustainable palm trade
[ALLIES]: Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam, Japan
[RED LINES]: No foreign naval bases within EEZ, non-interference in territorial integrity
[PAST UN ACTION]: Co-sponsor of UNCLOS implementation, voted in favor of UNGA Res 76/231

4. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Policy Anachronism: Representing your nation's historical stance from 1990 instead of its current recognized administration policy.
  • Personal Bias Intrusion: Allowing your personal humanitarian opinions to override the hardline strategic stance of your nation's government.
  • Fabricating Statistics: Citing unverified numbers during GSL speeches. Knowledgeable chairs and opponents will immediately raise points of order.

5. Actionable Research Checklist

  • ✓ Consult the committee background guide (study guide) thoroughly.
  • ✓ Identify 3 key UN resolutions previously passed on this agenda.
  • ✓ Note your country's voting position on those resolutions.
  • ✓ Identify 4 natural allies and 2 geopolitical rivals in your committee.
  • ✓ Prepare 3 actionable, funded solution mechanisms aligned with your foreign policy.
