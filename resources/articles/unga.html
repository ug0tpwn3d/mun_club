UN General Assembly (UNGA).

By SKIT MUN Editorial Team · 8 Min Read

As the primary deliberative, policymaking, and representative organ of the United Nations, the General Assembly embodies the principle of sovereign equality: one nation, one vote.

1. Structure and Main Committees

In full simulation or committee breakout formats, UNGA business is typically channeled through its six specialized Main Committees:

  • First Committee (DISEC): Disarmament and International Security. Focuses on nuclear non-proliferation, cyberwarfare, outer space militarization, and illicit arms trafficking.
  • Second Committee (ECOFIN): Economic and Financial. Addresses sovereign debt sustainability, macroeconomic stability, food security, and global climate financing mechanisms.
  • Third Committee (SOCHUM): Social, Humanitarian, and Cultural. Enforces refugee protection, indigenous rights, fundamental human freedoms, and international crime prevention.

2. Chamber Dynamics: The Law of Large Numbers

Unlike compact 15-member councils, an assembly chamber can house 50 to 120 delegates. Succeeding in the UNGA requires distinct tactical adaptations:

  • Placard Visibility: The Dais cannot call on every delegation. When raising your placard for the General Speakers List or moderated caucuses, raise it crisply at the exact moment the chair utters "All those in favor".
  • Microphone Projection: Deliver speeches with dynamic cadence and vocal volume. Address the entire room, turning between left, center, and right wings rather than speaking solely to the Executive Board.
  • Mass Regional Coalitions: A resolution cannot pass on the votes of European or North American nations alone. Winning delegates cultivate extensive alliances with African, Latin American, and Asia-Pacific regional caucuses.

3. The Uniting for Peace Resolution Precedent

Under UNGA Resolution 377A ("Uniting for Peace"), if the Security Council is paralyzed by a P5 veto during a grave threat to international peace, the General Assembly may convene an Emergency Special Session (ESS) and recommend collective measures—including military force—to restore global security. Understanding this legal mechanism showcases elite preparation in crisis moments.
