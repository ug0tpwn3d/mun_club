Continuous Crisis Committees.

By SKIT MUN Editorial Team · 8 Min Read

Crisis committees depart radically from traditional UN General Assembly debate. Instead of formal resolutions, delegates execute real-time directives, leverage portfolio powers, and navigate breaking geopolitical events simulated by a dedicated Backroom Crisis Cell.

1. Frontroom vs. Backroom Dynamics

In a Crisis simulation like the UNSC 1971 chamber at ARAMBH MUN:

  • The Frontroom: The public debating floor where delegates propose round-robin moderated discussions, public statements, and collaborative council directives.
  • The Backroom (Crisis Staff): The operational brain where delegates send private directives leveraging their character's distinct financial, military, diplomatic, or intelligence authority.

2. Structuring Effective Portfolio Directives

A successful private directive must be specific, realistic, and covert when necessary. It requires:

  1. Target / Objective: What specific outcome are you seeking to achieve?
  2. Assets Deployed: Which exact battalions, intelligence officers, or funds under your portfolio are you commanding?
  3. Operational Logic: How will the order be executed without leaking to adversarial portfolios?
  4. Contingency Planning: What happens if the primary line of communication fails?

3. Responding to Crisis Injects

When crisis directors enter the room with a televised bulletin or breaking dispatch, calm analysis triumphs over panic. Identify which actors are impacted, convene an emergency unmoderated caucus, and draft coordinated joint directives within minutes.
