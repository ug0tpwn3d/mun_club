MUN Dress Code & Diplomatic Poise.

By SKIT MUN Editorial Team · 6 Min Read

Diplomatic sartorial standards reflect respect for the international assemblies being simulated. Dressing properly establishes immediate credibility when you take the floor.

1. Western Business Formal (WBF)

Standard protocol for United Nations committees (UNSC, UNGA, UNHRC, UNICEF) requires strict Western Business Formal:

  • Gentlemen: Tailored two-piece or three-piece suits in navy blue, charcoal gray, or black. Solid or conservatively striped button-down dress shirts. Professional necktie or bow tie. Polished oxford, derby, or formal monk-strap dress shoes.
  • Ladies: Formal pantsuits, skirt suits (knee-length or longer), tailored blazers paired with formal trousers, or structured professional dresses. Neutral blouses. Closed-toe formal flats or comfortable low-to-mid heels.
  • Colors & Patterns: Stick to neutral, dignified palettes (navy, charcoal, slate gray, ivory, muted burgundy). Avoid neon shades, flashy casual prints, or sportswear materials.

2. Indian Traditional Formal (For Specialized Committees)

In Indian parliamentary committees such as the All India Political Parties Meet (AIPPM) and Lok Sabha, authentic Indian formal attire is welcomed and encouraged:

  • Men: Kurta-pajama or dhoti-kurta paired with a formal Nehru jacket (Modi jacket / bandhgala jacket). Clean leather formal mojris or dress shoes.
  • Women: Formal sarees, salwar kameez suits, or anarkalis in dignified textures and muted colors appropriate for parliamentary proceedings.

3. Diplomatic Decorum & Presence

  • Placard Handling: Keep your country placard flat and visible. Raise it decisively with a straight arm when voting or seeking speaking time; lower it when the chair acknowledges you.
  • Chamber Posture: Maintain an upright posture in your seat. Never slouch, sleep, or casually scroll smartphones in clear view of the dais.
  • Speech Delivery Stance: Step to the podium or stand behind your desk with feet shoulder-width apart. Maintain relaxed hand gestures and make contact with both wings of the chamber.
