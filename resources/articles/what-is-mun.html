What Is Model United Nations?

By SKIT MUN Editorial Team · 6 Min Read

Model United Nations (MUN) is an authentic academic simulation of the United Nations system, where students assume the roles of foreign diplomats to debate, negotiate, and formulate international policy.

1. The Core Philosophy of Simulation

Unlike traditional debate competitions where individuals contest abstract propositions from their personal perspectives, Model United Nations requires delegates to step completely into the sovereign persona of an assigned country or historical figure. A delegate representing the Republic of France must champion French foreign policy, regardless of their personal convictions.

2. How a MUN Chamber Functions

Every simulation is overseen by an Executive Board (EB), consisting of a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, and Rapporteur. The committee operates under a structured parliamentary flow:

  • Roll Call: Establishing committee quorum and voting status (Present or Present and Voting).
  • General Speakers List (GSL): The primary continuous agenda where nations articulate their overarching worldview.
  • Moderated Caucuses: Focused, time-bound debates on specific sub-issues of the agenda.
  • Unmoderated Caucuses: Informal lobbying periods for negotiation, bilateral consensus, and drafting resolutions.
  • Voting Bloc: Formal roll-call votes on working papers, amendments, and draft resolutions.

3. Why Participate in MUN at SKIT?

At SKIT MUN CLUB, we believe diplomacy is the ultimate interdisciplinary discipline. It sharpens policy research, analytical reading, impromptu articulation, and cross-cultural empathy—skills that distinguish our graduates across technological, management, and civil services careers.
