International Press Corps (IP).

The International Press Corps is the fourth estate of Model United Nations. Rather than representing a nation, IP journalists hold diplomats accountable through investigative reporting, incisive questioning, and editorial analysis.

1. Distinct Roles: Print Journalist vs. Photojournalist

Depending on your portfolio assignment within the Press Corps:

  • Print / Beat Journalist: Assigned to monitor specific committee chambers. You write breaking news bulletins, long-form op-eds, character profiles, and investigate backroom lobbying deals.
  • Photojournalist: Captures the decisive micro-moments of diplomacy: intense unmoderated arguments, solitary diplomats contemplating draft texts, and triumphant gavel strikes.

2. Adopting Your Publication's Editorial Voice

A journalist representing Reuters must write completely differently than one representing RT or The Guardian. Channeling the editorial slant of your assigned media house is central to award evaluation:

  • Reuters / Associated Press: Pure objective neutrality. Inverted-pyramid style. No emotional adjectives; focus purely on verified facts, attributed quotes, and economic indicators.
  • Al Jazeera English: Focuses heavily on the Global South, grassroots humanitarian impact, refugee perspectives, and challenging Western geopolitical dominance.
  • The Wall Street Journal / Financial Times: Analyzes committee deliberations through market impacts, trade sanctions, energy supply chains, and sovereign debt repercussions.

3. Conducting the Lethal Press Conference Question

During committee press conferences, journalists get 30 seconds to question delegates. Avoid weak questions like "What are you doing about peace?" Ask targeted, fact-anchored cross-examinations:

"Question for the Delegate of France from Reuters: In your opening address this morning, you stated that your government opposes all unvetted arms shipments. Yet official SIPRI trade records show that French naval defense firms finalized a $1.2B frigate transaction with the belligerent regime last quarter. How does the delegation reconcile this contradiction to the committee?"

4. The Golden Rules of MUN Journalism

  • Never misquote a delegate. Record accurate audio or jot exact written shorthand.
  • Do not disrupt ongoing caucuses. Circulate discreetly along the room perimeter.
  • Deliver your daily press dispatches and newsletter editions strictly before deadline.
