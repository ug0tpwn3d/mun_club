Frequently Asked Questions.

Everything you need to know about preparing for our conferences, understanding procedural mechanics, and securing your delegation.

The All India Political Parties Meet (AIPPM) is a parliamentary simulation in which participants represent Indian political parties or designated political portfolios. Delegates debate contemporary national issues, defend political positions, question opposing viewpoints, negotiate with other parties, and work towards practical policy or legislative solutions. Unlike conventional MUN committees, AIPPM focuses on Indian domestic politics, governance, legislation, constitutional considerations, and parliamentary discourse.
Yes. AIPPM is designed to accommodate both first-time participants and experienced delegates. You do not need prior parliamentary or MUN experience to participate. What matters most is your willingness to research, formulate arguments, listen to opposing perspectives, and speak with confidence. The format provides an opportunity to develop public speaking, critical thinking, negotiation, and spontaneous communication skills in a structured environment.
In AIPPM, you do not represent a foreign country. You represent an assigned Indian political party, political leader, or portfolio, depending on the committee's allocation system. Your speeches, arguments, and proposed policies should therefore remain reasonably consistent with the political position, ideology, public record, and responsibilities associated with your assigned representation.
Delegates are expected to maintain formal parliamentary decorum throughout the committee. Indian formal attire such as a formal Kurta-Pajama, Nehru Jacket, Bandhgala, Saree, or other appropriate traditional formal wear is suitable for AIPPM. Formal Western business attire is also acceptable. Delegates should maintain a professional appearance consistent with the seriousness of parliamentary debate.
AIPPM portfolios are allocated according to the registration and allocation procedure followed by the Secretariat. Delegates may be asked to submit portfolio preferences during registration, after which the Secretariat assigns available political parties or portfolios. Final allocations and relevant committee information are communicated to participants before the conference.
A Position Paper provides the foundation for your AIPPM preparation by outlining your assigned party or portfolio's understanding of the agenda, historical position, current stance, relevant legal and policy developments, and proposed solutions. Whether submission is mandatory, optional, or considered for awards will be determined by the Executive Board and Secretariat. Delegates should consult the official committee guidelines for the applicable requirements.
AIPPM commonly revolves around developing and debating legislative or policy proposals. Depending on the committee's Rules of Procedure, delegates may collaborate on a Bill or another formal policy document. These documents translate debate into concrete measures by addressing the problem, proposing reforms, identifying implementation mechanisms, and considering legal, administrative, financial, and political implications.
No. AIPPM is not about speaking flawless English or using complicated vocabulary. Effective public speaking is about conveying your thoughts clearly, structuring an argument, responding to others, and communicating with confidence. AIPPM is designed to give participants the opportunity to improve these skills through debate, discussion, negotiation, questioning, and spontaneous speaking.
Delegates are evaluated on factors such as quality of arguments, research, parliamentary participation, negotiation, responsiveness, consistency with their assigned portfolio, and overall performance in committee. Awards may include Best Delegate, High Commendation, Special Mention, or other recognitions as determined by the Executive Board and Secretariat.
