On the discipline of
measured speech.

Dear Delegate,

When we first walked into a Model United Nations committee room, what struck us was not the size of the arguments but the quality of the listening. Diplomacy is not the art of winning a debate — it is the art of building a position so considered, so grounded in fact and empathy, that even your opposition pauses to recalibrate.

ARAMBH MUN was built on a simple conviction: that every student from this institution — regardless of their branch, their semester, or their prior MUN experience — deserves a room where their voice carries weight. Not because they were born fluent in procedure, but because they chose to learn. That choice is already half the credential.

"The floor is not given to the loudest. It is earned by those who come prepared, speak with precision, and stay curious beyond their own clause."
— Dr. Shiv Priya, Faculty Co-ordinator

We have watched SKIT MUN grow from an informal reading group of twelve students to a conference with 40+ delegates and a reputation that travels beyond Jaipur. What has not changed — and what we actively protect — is the culture of rigour inside the committee room. We do not award speaking time to titles. We award it to preparation.

To every delegate attending ARAMBH MUN 2026: read your study guide twice. Source your claims. Disagree on record. And when the chair calls for a moderated caucus, do not stand at the podium to restate your bloc's position — stand there to move the room.

We look forward to watching you rise.

Dr. Shiv Priya & Mrs. Sanju Chaudhary

From the student team:
why this matters.

To everyone who registered for ARAMBH MUN 2026,

We built this conference from inside a classroom. The logistics spreadsheet, the study guide, the committee matrix, the crisis update feed — every line of it was written by students who had once sat exactly where you are sitting: reading about MUN for the first time, unsure whether they belonged in the room.

You do. That is the point of ARAMBH MUN. It has always been a conference that takes its delegates seriously from the moment they sign up, not just after they have won an award. Our adjudication core is trained to reward genuine engagement — a well-sourced objection, a creative amendment, an honest admission that a bloc position has a flaw. Performing confidence is easy. Demonstrating intellectual honesty is rare.

"Every portfolio is a passport. The question is not where it takes you — it's how far you're willing to go with it."
— Nishant Kumar, Student Co-ordinator

We have made some changes for 2026. Crisis updates will be issued every 45 minutes in the UNSC — 1971 room. The Lok Sabha committee will follow a hybrid procedure that blends formal MUN rules with parliamentary motion formats. And for the first time, we are running a post-conference delegate debrief open to all participants.

We are proud of what this club has become. We are even more curious about what you will make of it on October 23rd.

Nishant Kumar, Keshav Saini, Shivansh Dwivedi & Ronak Dev

Conference Note

On the theme:
ARAMBH.

The name we chose is not incidental. Arambh — a Sanskrit word meaning beginning, initiation, the first act of a new order — captures exactly what we believe every committee session should feel like. Not a rehearsal of positions already held, but a genuine beginning of something the room did not expect.

The committees for 2026 — UNGA DISEC, UNHRC, UNSC 1971, and Lok Sabha Special Session — were chosen because they represent different layers of international and domestic governance. A delegate who can move between the abstraction of global security architecture and the procedural texture of parliamentary debate is a delegate who understands why the two are inseparable.

We ask every delegate, regardless of experience level, to approach their committee with the same orientation: what does this room need to move forward, and how can my position help it get there? Awards follow preparation. Preparation follows curiosity.

The floor will open at 09:00 on October 23rd, 2026.

Order. Order. The delegate may speak.
